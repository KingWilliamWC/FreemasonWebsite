Freemasonry

What Is Freemasonry?

Freemasonry is one of the world's oldest fraternal organisations, centred on friendship, personal development, integrity and charity.

At its heart, Freemasonry brings people together in a spirit of respect, self-improvement and service to others. It is not about status or secrecy for its own sake, but about character, fellowship and shared values.

An Introduction

A tradition built around values and fellowship

Freemasonry encourages its members to live thoughtfully, act honestly and contribute positively to the communities around them. It uses tradition, symbolism and ceremony as a way of teaching moral lessons and encouraging reflection.

For many members, the appeal lies in the combination of friendship, structure, continuity and purpose. Lodge life brings together people from different backgrounds who may otherwise never have met, creating a strong sense of belonging and mutual support.

While Freemasonry has a long history, it remains relevant because the values it encourages are timeless: honesty, respect, responsibility, charity and care for others.

Core Principles

Integrity

Trying to act honourably, speak truthfully and carry responsibilities seriously.

Friendship

Building strong bonds across different ages, professions and backgrounds.

Charity

Supporting good causes and helping others through practical generosity.

Self Improvement

Encouraging reflection, learning and personal growth throughout life.

Common Questions

What Freemasonry is and is not

People often first encounter Freemasonry through myths, half-truths or second-hand ideas. In reality, it is far more straightforward and grounded.

It is a fraternity

Freemasonry is a society built around membership, shared values and regular meetings, where people come together in a respectful setting.

It is not a secret power

Although some traditions and ceremonies are private to members, Freemasonry is not a hidden authority or political movement.

It supports charity

Charity and service are central to Masonic life, both through organised giving and through support within local communities.

Enquiring Locally

What to expect if you want to learn more

If you are curious about Freemasonry, the first step is simply to ask. There is no expectation of prior knowledge. A genuine interest and a willingness to understand the organisation are enough.

A local lodge such as St John In Bedwardine Lodge can explain what its meetings are like, what membership involves and whether it feels like the right fit for you.
